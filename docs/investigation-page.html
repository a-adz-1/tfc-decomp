1The short version

Most of the "jank" is not random. It comes from a few fixed rules in the game and engine that react badly to frame timing and network delay.

Netcode

The server sends updates on whole server frames, rounded up. Unlucky settings halve your updates (e.g. cl_updaterate 100 on a 100-tick server gives ~50 a second).

Sentry guns

Pick the first player by slot number, not the nearest; see you with one eye-to-eye line; only notice mid-range players inside their current sweep; and get no lag compensation.

Ramp slides

The same ramp at the same speed slides about twice as far at some fps as at others, and at very high fps players can stop dead on the ramp.

25th Anniversary

Netcode, movement and sentries are the same as legacy. What it broke is pushing: push fields and pushable crates now depend on server frame rate.

2Netcode: updates and commands

Two streams matter: the server's updates to you (net_graph "in") and your commands to the server (net_graph "out"). The timing rules are identical in the legacy and current engines, on both server and client.

Server → you

Each server frame, the server sends you an update if your update interval (1 ÷ cl_updaterate) has passed, then schedules the next one from that frame. So the real gap between updates is your interval rounded up to whole server frames.

The server doesn't sit idle between updates. It still runs every frame (about 840 a second on the OTFC pickup server): moving everyone, running grenades and sentries, and taking in your commands the moment they arrive. Only the update sent to you waits, because the game caps you at 102 a second. Each update carries the newest state at that moment, so nothing in between is lost; it just reaches you in ~10.7 ms steps instead of ~9.8 ms. On a 100-tick server the frames themselves are 10 ms apart, so everything is that coarse.

  • That is where the "always use 102" advice comes from: 100 almost never fits a whole number of frames.
  • The game itself caps cl_updaterate at 102. A server's sv_maxupdaterate above that does nothing.
  • The server only behaves on a steady, fine frame rate, which on Linux needs the -pingboost start option.

You → server

  • Your game builds a command every frame, and the command's length carries the leftover fraction of a millisecond to the next one, so no movement time is lost.
  • It sends them by the same round-up rule on your fps. With fps_max 100 and cl_cmdrate 100 it flips between every frame and every other frame. Set cl_cmdrate a little above your fps.
  • The send timer uses a 32-bit float of time since the game started. It gets coarser the longer the game stays open: about 1 ms after 3 hours, about 8 ms after a day. Restarting resets it.

3Sentry gun lock-on

The sentry thinks every 0.1 s. While idle it checks players in slot order; the first one that passes becomes its target, and it keeps that target until it becomes invalid.

What players noticeWhy
It ignores the soldier in its face and keeps shooting someone far awayTarget = first valid player by slot number, not the nearest. It never re-picks while the current one is still valid
Same spot, sometimes it locks and sometimes notUnder 500 units it sees all around. From 500 to 1000 units it only sees you within ~45° of where the turret is pointing right now, and it is always sweeping ±50°
Head out = shot, body out = fine (or the reverse over a ledge)Visibility is one line from the sentry's eye to your eyes. Nothing else of your body counts. Crouching moves your eyes
Reaction time is all over the placeIt only fires once its aim is within 10°. Turning speeds up gradually from whatever speed the idle sweep left. Behind it: ~1.4 s; just off the barrel: almost instant
Concing players above or below are slow to get hitUp/down aim moves a fixed 3° per think with no speed-up. A target 40° above buys about a second
It turns at different speeds on different serversAll turning assumes exactly 0.1 s between thinks. Coarse server frames make thinks late and uneven

Server side vs what you see

  • No lag compensation for sentries. The server rewinds players for hit checks only when a player shoots. A sentry shoots where you are on the server right now. Your screen is ahead of that by roughly half your ping, so you get hit after you have already reached cover on your screen.
  • You see the turret late. Its aim reaches you half a ping late and is then shown with interpolation delay, so "it locked on before it faced me" is often just that.

None of this changed in the 25th Anniversary update.

4Ramp slides and fps

We measured on the real engine and game code: a scout arriving in the air at the foot of an up-ramp, no keys held, at different client frame rates. Movement only depends on your fps (your command lengths), not the server's tick rate.

Height slid up the ramp, in units. The same ramp and speed lands on one of two heights, roughly double or half, depending on fps. At 1000 fps it can stop almost at once.

Three causes

  1. How you meet the ramp. Hit it while still in the air and the part of your speed going into the slope is lost (you keep about 87% on 30°, 77% on 40°). Touch the floor for even one frame just before it and the ground movement steps you onto the slope with all your speed. Which happens depends on where your frames fall, so it flips with fps and with timing luck.
  2. Gravity per frame. Lower fps takes bigger gravity steps between the "rising faster than 180 units/s = not on the ground" checks, so you count as landed sooner and friction starts.
  3. The ramp bug. At very high fps each frame's move is tiny, and the collision check, which keeps you 1/32 unit off surfaces, reports "blocked" while you slide along the slope. The movement code then gives up and zeroes your speed: a dead stop.

The 25th Anniversary game code gives the same table to within ~2 units. This is how TFC has always moved; the update neither caused nor fixed it.

Nothing about sentries or ramp physics is a regression. They are how TFC has always behaved. The netcode is the 2000 rebuild, unchanged. "fps_max 100 is best" probably dates from the 2001 client, which lost time at frame rates that don't divide 1000. The big change since then is frame rates: modern clients run far above 100 fps, which is where the ramp bug appears, and modern servers can run a steady high tick, which makes updates better than they were.

7Rendering: software, Direct3D, OpenGL

The renderer is the part of your game that draws the picture. It never changes hit detection or the movement rules; hits are checked on the server. But it sets your frame rate, which changes your movement and how many commands you send, and it changes what you see.

So the old "72 fps" was the 1999 default, 100 has been the cap since 2000, and going past 100 needs fps_override 1. We only compared Linux builds of today's client, so whether the current Windows client still has Direct3D is open.

Why it can feel choppy on modern hardware

Monitoring tools measure how evenly your PC draws frames, and on that the game is genuinely good: while it has focus it never sleeps, it spins and starts the next frame the moment its frame cap allows, so frame times are very even. The choppiness comes from places those tools don't look.

CauseWhat happens
Frame rate vs refresh rate100 fps on a 144, 165 or 240 Hz screen without G-Sync/FreeSync can't line up: frames stay up for one refresh, then two, unevenly. That judder is invisible to frame-time graphs. Back in the day 72–100 fps on a 72–100 Hz CRT lined up, and CRTs flash each frame instead of holding it, so motion looked crisper than on today's hold-type LCDs at the same fps
The cap is half a frame too fastfps_max N really caps at N + 0.5. Set it to your refresh rate (e.g. 144 on 144 Hz, with fps_override 1) and you produce one extra frame every 2 seconds: a regular hitch with vsync on, a slowly crawling tear line with it off
Vsync on by default (25th)Adds buffering delay, and when your fps is below the refresh rate frames wait for the next refresh: uneven and late
The game clock keeps being correctedEvery update from the server carries the server's time. Your game compares it with its own clock and pulls its clock towards it by up to cl_fixtimerate (7.5 ms) per frame. Uneven update arrival (network jitter, coarse server frames) becomes little speed-ups and slow-downs in how other players move, while your own frame times stay perfect
Other players update ~93–100 times a secondYour screen shows 144–240 frames, so other players are always blended between updates. Too little ex_interp (section 8) and the blending runs out
Your own movement changes with fpsAbove 100 fps your ramp slides and the ramp bug change (section 4), which feels like inconsistency even when the picture is smooth

Graphics drivers

These are known platform behaviours rather than things we measured, but they fit the symptoms.

  • Old-style OpenGL. The game draws with 1990s-style OpenGL (the 25th adds a shader path). Drivers today are tuned for DirectX and Vulkan, and their latency features (NVIDIA Reflex / Low Latency Mode, AMD Anti-Lag) don't apply to OpenGL games. That's why the 25th added its own gl_lowlatency.
  • Windows, windowed or borderless: OpenGL frames often go through the desktop compositor, which adds a frame of delay and can stop G-Sync/FreeSync working. Use true fullscreen. On NVIDIA, the control panel's "Vulkan/OpenGL present method: Prefer layered on DXGI swapchain" puts OpenGL on the modern present path, and turning "Threaded optimization" off is worth trying for old OpenGL games.
  • AMD and Intel on Windows: their OpenGL drivers were historically weaker than NVIDIA's (AMD replaced theirs in 2022); fullscreen and up-to-date drivers matter more there.
  • Linux: the desktop compositor can add a frame of delay (fullscreen or a direct-scanout setup avoids it). Vsync is often forced by the driver (Mesa vblank_mode, NVIDIA __GL_SYNC_TO_VBLANK), and on NVIDIA __GL_MaxFramesAllowed=1 cuts queued frames.
Read the full explanation →

Smoothest setup: true fullscreen; with G-Sync/FreeSync, fps_override 1 and fps_max a few below your refresh rate; without it, gl_vsync 0. Try gl_lowlatency 1. cl_fixtimerate below 7.5 (e.g. 2–3) should make other players' motion steadier at the cost of slower clock syncing; we haven't tested it in game. And remember the trade-off: above 100 fps the picture is smoother but ramp slides behave differently.

8Choppiness and getting stuck

Getting stuck on players

  • Teammates are solid. TFC lets everything collide with everything, so you block teammates and they block you.
  • You collide with other players where you saw them. When the server runs your movement it first rewinds everyone else to where they were on your screen (the same lag compensation used for shots), checks your collisions against those positions, then puts them back. Every player is colliding with everyone else's past, so two players running into each other can end up overlapping in the present.
  • Once overlapped, the server freezes you. A player whose box is inside something gets no movement at all for that command. The server only retries a small shove every 0.05 s, and the first 27 tries are too small to be used, so it can take over a second. Your own game (the 25th client) tries ducking and searches for a free spot straight away, so on your screen you walk free, then the server pulls you back: rubber-banding.

Tip: stuck inside another player? Hold jump, duck or fire. The server then searches nearby spots for a free one immediately instead of waiting.

Getting stuck on walls and ramps

  • Corners stop you dead. When a move hits three surfaces at once (an acute corner, a wall meeting a slope), the movement code gives up and sets your speed to zero.
  • The ramp bug is the same rule misfiring. The code records every surface it bumps in a move without checking for repeats. With tiny moves (high fps) it can bump the same slope twice; two identical surfaces count as a corner whose direction is zero, so your speed becomes zero. That is the dead stop in section 4.
  • Pushed into a wall by a door, lift or blast, you are inside solid, and the slow server unstick above applies.

13Conclusion

TFC isn't janky at random. Most of it comes down to three things: the engine rounding updates and commands to whole frames, sentry guns using simple rules (slot order, one sight line, a sweeping view cone, no lag compensation), and movement that depends on your frame rate.

The networking part is fixed with settings, and the OTFC servers now run them: precise frames with -pingboost, a high tick, and everyone on 102 updates a second. On your side: cl_updaterate 102, cl_cmdrate above your fps, and fps_max 100 if you ramp slide.

The 25th Anniversary update didn't change any of this. Its real damage was pushing, which is why OTFC keeps the legacy game code while players can still use the newer client's extras.

Comparing with the original WON-era versions (1999–2003) shows the sentry and ramp behaviour have been there since the start, and the netcode is the 2000 rebuild, unchanged. So fixing them means changing TFC, not restoring it.

What's left needs code: a ramp-bug fix we can add with little risk, and sentry changes that alter balance, which the community should try and vote on.
